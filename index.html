Research

A robot can do more when its mechanism does part of the work. My research builds parallel robots in which grasping, unlimited rotation and redundancy are produced by the structure itself, with every motor fixed to the base, instead of by devices added at the tip. I take each idea from theory to hardware: type synthesis, kinematics, statics and singularity analysis; simulation and mechanical design; then fabrication and real-time multi-motor control of the prototype.

Geometric Foundations

Screw theory and line geometry for type synthesis, singularity factorisation and design rules that hold across whole families of robots.

Robots that Grasp and Turn

Kinematically redundant parallel robots with articulated platforms: unlimited rotations about one, two or three axes, and grippers driven through the mechanism.

Physical Intelligence

Bodies that sense and act: grasp force estimated from motor currents, wheels that also manipulate, and simulation-driven co-design of mechanism and control.

Where I want to take this next

  • Morphology as a design variable for learning. Use analytical measures (singularity distance, conditioning, grasp indices) to make joint search over robot bodies and control policies in simulation tractable.
  • Manipulation where actuators cannot go. Base-actuated mechanisms for underwater, sterile or confined workspaces, where motors near the tool are a liability.
  • Proprioceptive interaction. Sensing contact and grasp forces through the mechanism and its motors, without force sensors at the tool.

Ph.D. Dissertation

Kinematically Redundant Parallel Robots with Articulated Platforms for Unlimited Rotation and Grasping

Université Laval · Supervisor: Prof. Clément Gosselin · Co-supervisor: Prof. Patrice Lambert · Thesis by publication, defense January 2027

Parallel robots are stiff, fast and precise, but their rotations are usually limited to a few tens of degrees and they cannot grasp without a gripper and motor mounted on the moving platform. The thesis removes both limits at once. It proposes base-actuated architectures, from a compact 4-DoF leg and a coaxial wrist-gripper to (6+1)- and (6+2)-DoF robots, in which a passive joint inside the platform turns redundancy into grasping and unlimited rotation. It contributes a classification of grasping parallel robots, an algebraic framework for collision-free unlimited rotation, a general factorisation of Type II singularities for articulated platforms, a reduced graspability Jacobian, and experimental validation on physical prototypes.

Built and tested

Real prototype
Prototype · MassRobotics finalist 2026

(6+2)-DoF robot with three orthogonal unlimited rotations

Eight legs driven by coaxial base motors through two arms that end in bars on passive joints of the platform. The platform can make complete turns about each of its three axes while keeping its three translations, a combination not reported before for a parallel robot.

  • 9-motor prototype, real-time control on OPAL-RT RT-LAB
  • Video: hardware test at 3× speed
  • Collision-free turns validated in MuJoCo on the real CAD meshes
  • Built with the two graduate students I supervise
  • Finalist, MassRobotics Form & Function Challenge 2026 (Boston)

Under review, ICRA 2027

Real prototype
Prototype · validated

Compact coaxial parallel wrist-gripper

Three orientation DoF plus grasping, with all four motors on the base driving concentric gear rings. The gripper shaft runs through the central universal joint, so the tool rolls without limit and no cable twists, while a non-backdrivable stage keeps the object held.

  • 180 mm footprint, 0.25 kg moving mass
  • >10 continuous revolutions holding an object
  • Joint RMS error < 0.1°, aperture held within ±0.05°; 500 Hz control

ConGrip: concentric (6+1)-DoF robot with grasping

Seven base-mounted actuators with kinematic and actuation redundancy. The gripper is driven from the base through a concentric tube, so the robot can grasp, reorient without bound and regrasp with no motor on the moving platform.

  • 7 actuators, all on the base
  • Regrasping and tilted-platform tasks planned in MuJoCo

Prototype under construction

Four-leg Delta robot with an integrated Sarrus gripper

The platform is split into two plates joined by a Sarrus linkage; their relative motion closes parallel jaws that stay at constant height. Grasp force is estimated from motor currents, to handle fragile objects at speed without a force sensor.

  • 4 DoF (x, y, z, grip), all motors on the base
  • Dynamic model in MuJoCo and Simscape before hardware
  • Simulated grip-force estimation error 0.06 N

Award-winning design

(6+2)-DoF robot with two orthogonal unlimited rotations and remote grasping

Two 4-DoF sub-robots drive a platform with an internal bevel-gear train: moving the redundant inputs together turns the gripper without limit, moving them against each other opens and closes it. First place, ASME SMRDC 2025. The design is now being adapted for underwater manipulation.

Exploratory projects

Simulation
In development

Mobile robots whose wheels also manipulate

Ongoing work on mobile robots in which parallel mechanisms driven by the wheel motors level a payload, grasp objects and estimate external forces, with no extra actuators or force sensors. Kinematics, statics and model-based controllers are developed in MuJoCo, together with design sweeps and parametric CAD with automatic collision checks.

  • Early simulation: crosses a 20 cm crater with the load tilted ≤ 0.4°
  • Early simulation: rejects a 50 N push, estimating it within 4 %
Model
Exploratory · design

Print-in-place compliant Delta robot with gripper

A one-piece PLA/TPU Delta robot with flexure joints and four driving cables. Pseudo-rigid-body stiffness model, cable statics, and cable anchors optimised by differential evolution to maximise the feasible workspace.
